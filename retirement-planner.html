Key figures at retirement

This is a simplified educational model, not financial or actuarial advice. The DC-vs-DB comparison above is pre-tax throughout. Only the goal check is after-tax: it runs the same tax-optimized withdrawal engine as the tax-optimized withdrawal page for year one only — same province (Ontario by default; change it on that page), same bracket-filling order, same income-splitting and OAS clawback handling — so it should always agree with that page's first row. Otherwise this page ignores income tax on withdrawals, capital-gains treatment on non-registered stock, RRSP/TFSA contribution-room limits, and early-retirement reductions. DC and RRSP balances (and a spouse's, if married) are combined into one registered pool for retirement drawdown, using a blended return — TFSA, stocks, cash and SERP form a second pool that always draws down at the chosen withdrawal rate here, since this page doesn't model tax at all (on the tax-optimized withdrawal page, SERP instead pays out as a taxable lump sum in your first retirement year, with after-tax proceeds added to cash — this page approximates that by having SERP earn cash's return from retirement on). CPP and OAS are estimated from the amounts you enter (not calculated from a contribution history), actuarially adjusted for your chosen start age, and added equally to both the savings and theoretical-DB income lines since you'd receive them regardless of pension type — OAS clawback is not applied here. If married, retirement age and income horizon are assumed shared between spouses, and the theoretical DB remains yours alone. The theoretical DB figure applies a generic Canadian public-sector-style formula (accrual rate × years of service × final average earnings) purely as a comparison yardstick — it is not a quote from, or model of, any actual pension plan. Contribution and return assumptions compound every year without accounting for market volatility. Speak with a qualified financial planner before making retirement decisions.

This withdrawal order is a simplified rule of thumb, not a tax optimization or professional advice. Your goal (set on the planner page) is treated as an after-tax target, funded from your real accounts and benefits — the theoretical DB pension is a hypothetical comparison and isn't included here. SERP pays out as a single taxable lump sum in your first year of retirement (not drawn down gradually), taxed alongside that year's other income; the after-tax proceeds are added to cash from then on. Each year otherwise draws mandatory income first (CPP, OAS, and RRIF minimums from age 71), then tops up any after-tax shortfall from RRSP/RRIF — grossed up by the marginal rate at each step so the after-tax result lands on target, while staying within the current combined federal+provincial bracket — then cash, then non-registered stock, then TFSA last (both tax-free here, so no grossing needed). The grossing-up math is exact within one bracket step and approximate if a single year's shortfall spans more than two brackets, and it does not account for OAS clawback when sizing withdrawals (clawback is still applied to the final tax figure, so after-tax results can land slightly under goal in clawback-affected ranges). Non-registered and cash withdrawals are treated as tax-free principal withdrawals here — in reality, capital gains and dividends in a non-registered account are taxable, which this page does not model. The basic personal amount is modeled as a simple deduction rather than the real credit mechanism, which affects precision — Québec also has its own federal-tax abatement not modeled here. This is a heuristic, not a true optimizer: an actual optimizer could sequence withdrawals differently to reduce lifetime tax further. Speak with a qualified tax professional or financial planner before making withdrawal decisions.
